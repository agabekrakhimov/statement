# Build Prompt: Fleet Settlement Analytics Dashboard

Copy everything below the line into a new chat (with Claude, or any capable
coding agent) to brief it on building the production version of this
product. It's written from the analysis of 5 real weekly settlement
statements for a one-truck owner-operator running under carrier "Wenze
Transport Services" (statements generated by datatruck.io).

---

## Prompt

Build a web app for an owner-operator / small trucking fleet that ingests
weekly "Truck Owner Settlement" and "Driver Pay Settlement" PDF statements
(the kind generated by dispatch/back-office platforms like datatruck.io,
Axle, Truckbase, Rose Rocket, etc.) and turns them into a running financial
and operational dashboard.

### Who uses it

An owner-operator or small fleet owner (1–20 trucks) who gets one PDF per
truck per week from their carrier/dispatcher. They currently just file
these away. They want to upload the PDF every week (from a phone at a
truck stop, ideally) and immediately see whether each truck is making
money, where the money is going, and which truck needs attention.

### Core workflow

1. **Upload** — drag/drop or phone-camera-to-PDF upload of one or more
   statement PDFs, any time, no fixed schedule required (the app derives
   the week from the statement's own period dates, and is idempotent: the
   same PDF re-uploaded is deduped, not double-counted).
2. **Parse** — extract structured data from the PDF (see data model
   below). Because statement layouts vary by carrier/software, use a
   two-stage approach: (a) a fast template-matched parser for known
   layouts, (b) a fallback that shows the raw extracted text next to an
   editable form so the user can correct/complete fields in under a
   minute when parsing is imperfect. Never silently drop a statement
   because parsing failed — always fall through to manual review.
3. **Reconcile** — detect duplicate or conflicting statements (same
   statement ID / same truck+period uploaded twice with different
   numbers) and surface a diff instead of silently overwriting.
4. **Analyze** — compute the metrics and insights below automatically,
   per truck and per driver, updated every time a new statement lands.
5. **Review** — a dashboard with trend charts, a truck leaderboard, and a
   plain-English "what changed and what needs attention this week"
   summary, plus a running log of flagged issues to resolve.

### Data model to extract per statement

- Header: carrier name, statement/bill ID, truck/unit number, driver
  name, period start/end, bill date, check date, trip count.
- Owner settlement: total gross revenue, total driver pay, owner gross
  bill, deductions (itemized by type — company fee %, trailer rental,
  insurance, IFTA, ELD, escrow, maintenance line items with invoice
  ID/date/description, fuel, other), net pay to owner.
- Driver settlement: driver earnings by load, advances, reimbursements,
  fines/violations (with description/date), other pay (e.g. escrow
  returns), net pay to driver, YTD figures.
- Load/trip detail: load number, pickup city/state, delivery city/state,
  pickup date, delivery date, loaded miles, empty (deadhead) miles, total
  miles, gross revenue, driver payment, rate/mile.
- Any balances/holdbacks shown (escrow running balance, fuel card
  balance owed, reserve accounts).

### Metrics to compute (per truck, per week, and rolling)

- **Revenue per mile (RPM)** = gross revenue / total miles, and **loaded
  RPM** = gross revenue / loaded miles only (separates deadhead drag from
  true linehaul rate).
- **Net RPM** = owner net pay / total miles — the actual profitability
  signal, not just gross.
- **Deadhead %** = empty miles / total miles, trended over time and
  flagged above a configurable threshold (default 15%).
- **Cost per mile, broken into categories**: company/dispatch fee,
  insurance, trailer rental, IFTA, ELD, maintenance, fuel, escrow, other
  — each as $/mile so trucks of different weekly mileage are comparable.
- **Maintenance cost**: weekly total, trailing-4-week average, and
  spike detection (a week's maintenance > 2x trailing average), plus a
  running cost-per-mile-since-last-major-service view once odometer data
  is available.
- **Fuel economy (MPG)** — see "What you're missing" below; only
  computable with gallons data, not dollars alone. Show an *estimated*
  MPG (fuel $ ÷ assumed price/gal ÷ miles) clearly labeled as an estimate
  with an editable diesel price assumption, and a prominent prompt to
  connect real fuel-card data for accurate numbers.
- **Margin %** = net pay / gross revenue, trended.
- **Driver pay vs. owner pay split**, and a **fines/violations log**
  (compliance issues hit driver pay, not owner P&L, but should still be
  tracked — repeated violations affect CSA score and future insurance).
- **Truck leaderboard**: rank all trucks by net RPM (and by margin %)
  for the trailing 4/8/12 weeks, clearly separating structural
  differences (a truck that just had a $1,800 tire replacement isn't
  "underperforming," it made a capital investment) from a truck with
  chronically high deadhead or low RPM.

### "Deep analysis" the dashboard must surface automatically

Not just charts — plain-language findings, e.g.:
- "Truck 777's net RPM dropped from $1.82 to $0.32 this week — driven by
  a $1,774 tire purchase (5 drive tires), not a rate or lane problem."
- "Truck 777 ran 24.8% deadhead miles this week (vs. 3% trailing
  average) — the Port Chester → Port Chester load looks like a short
  drayage move bracketed by a long empty leg; worth a note to dispatch."
- "Two statements were uploaded for BL-000795 with different insurance
  deductions ($400 vs $200) and different net pay — flagged for you to
  confirm which is correct with the carrier."
- "Driver Damarion had a $450 violation deduction the week of 7/21 —
  logged to the compliance tab."
- "No fuel gallons data available for any week — MPG figures are
  estimates only. Connect a fuel card feed or log gallons manually to
  unlock real fuel-efficiency tracking."

### Visualizations

- Multi-truck line chart: Net RPM (and Revenue RPM) over time, one line
  per truck, so over/under-performers are visually obvious.
- Stacked area/bar: cost-per-mile by deduction category, over time.
- Line chart: maintenance $ per week with trailing average band.
- Bar chart: deadhead % per week, with threshold line.
- Table: per-truck, per-week raw numbers, sortable/exportable to CSV.
- A driver-pay view mirroring the above for compliance/payroll.

### Non-functional requirements

- Mobile-friendly upload (photographed/scanned PDFs from a truck stop).
- Works with imperfect/varying PDF layouts — never a hard failure, always
  degrade to manual-entry.
- Multi-truck, multi-driver from day one (a driver can move between
  trucks; a truck can change drivers — track both dimensions).
- Exportable data (CSV/JSON) for tax prep (owner-operators are usually
  1099/Schedule C) and for handing to an accountant.
- Historical data should keep accumulating indefinitely (weekly cadence,
  years of history) — the value compounds over time.

### Suggested stack

Any is fine, but a pragmatic default: a small backend (Node/Express or
Python/FastAPI) with Postgres/SQLite for storage, `pdf-parse`/`pdfplumber`
or a hosted PDF-to-text service for extraction, a template-based
extraction layer keyed by carrier (fall back to an LLM-assisted extractor
for unrecognized layouts if available), and a React frontend with a
charting library (Recharts/Chart.js) for the dashboard.

## What you're likely missing (from analyzing your actual 5 statements)

1. **Fuel gallons and price/gallon.** The statements only show a lump
   dollar "Fuel" balance ($1,836.90 in one), never gallons purchased,
   price paid, or odometer at fill-up. Without this, true MPG cannot be
   calculated — only estimated. Get the fuel card export (EFS, Comdata,
   WEX, RTS, TCH, etc.) which includes gallons, price/gal, location, and
   often odometer per transaction.
2. **Odometer readings.** Neither weekly nor at fill-up. These would let
   you cross-check dispatch miles vs. actual miles driven (catching
   unreported/personal miles) and compute real MPG.
3. **IFTA quarterly fuel tax reports.** You're already paying $40/week
   for IFTA — the underlying quarterly filing has gallons purchased and
   miles driven by state, which is a free, already-collected source of
   fuel data you're not currently using for MPG tracking.
4. **Truck-level fixed costs not on these statements**: truck note/lease
   payment, physical damage insurance, ELD hardware cost, permits/plates,
   parking, tolls, scale fees, per diem. Without these your "net pay"
   from the carrier statement isn't your true owner P&L — it's revenue
   minus the carrier's cut, not minus your own fixed costs.
5. **Telematics/ELD data** (idle time, engine hours, hard-braking,
   speeding, harsh acceleration). This is what actually separates "truck
   problem" from "driver problem" when fuel economy or wear looks bad —
   right now truck and driver are bundled into one settlement.
6. **Vehicle identity/maintenance history**: year/make/model/VIN, mileage
   at each service, a PM (preventive maintenance) schedule. The
   statements list maintenance line items with invoice IDs but nothing
   ties them to a service interval or predicts the next one.
7. **Load/lane detail beyond what's billed**: broker name, detention pay,
   layover, dispatch/booking fee if any. Needed to find out which lanes
   or brokers are actually the profitable ones, not just which weeks.
8. **An external benchmark.** "Outperforming" is currently only relative
   to your own other trucks/weeks. Industry $/mile and MPG benchmarks
   (by truck age/class) would tell you if even your "best" truck is
   actually good.
9. **Unit/label normalization.** The same physical truck appears as
   "777 Damarion" in early statements and "777 OG'ABEK" later (label
   changed when... possibly a driver change or naming convention switch).
   The app must map these to one truck record, not silently create two.
10. **Duplicate/conflicting statements.** You uploaded two versions of
    BL-000795 with different insurance deductions ($400 vs. $200) and
    different net pay ($368.92 vs. $568.92). The tool needs to catch this
    and ask you which is authoritative rather than picking one silently.
11. **Escrow account tracking.** $250/week is being escrowed with no
    visible running balance, cap, or refund event in these documents —
    worth tracking as its own line so you know what you're owed back.
12. **A compliance log**, separate from the P&L: the $450 "violation"
    deduction against the driver's pay doesn't touch your owner
    settlement directly, but repeated violations affect CSA score and
    future insurance costs — worth its own trend, not buried in payroll.
13. **Normalized (per-mile, per-trip) views, not just weekly totals.**
    Trip count varies week to week (1, 3, 2, 3) so raw weekly totals are
    noisy; $/mile and $/trip are the comparable numbers.
14. **Tax-ready exports.** As a 1099 owner-operator you'll want quarterly
    gross/net and categorized expenses ready for Schedule C / quarterly
    estimated tax payments — this can fall out of the same data for free.
